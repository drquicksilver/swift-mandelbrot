Executive summary

This is a genuinely good fractal explorer wrapped in an unfinished application. The parts that touch the fractal directly — the canvas, the gestures, the palettes, the Julia companion, the Mac movie dialog — are excellent and in places better than commercial equivalents. Almost everything that surrounds them is a single generic SwiftUI Form inside a NavigationStack, shipped unchanged to both platforms, and on the Mac that shared chrome is visibly broken.

What already works well

  • The fractal is the hero. On both platforms the canvas is edge to edge, the chrome is a single translucent cluster, and nothing competes with the image. Launching the app and pinching is exactly the experience the vision document asks for.
  • Direct manipulation is first class. One transform solves pan, zoom and rotate together from the raw touches, so the point under each finger stays pinned; the Mac gets trackpad pinch, twist, scroll-to-zoom-at-pointer, shift-drag framing and inertial flings. This is the hardest part of the product and it is done properly.
  • The Julia companion on macOS is a delight. The crosshair follows the pointer and the companion re-renders live. It makes the relationship between the two sets tangible in a way few apps manage.
  • The Mac Zoom Movie dialog is a model of what the rest of the app should look like — a real dialog with a title, cards, thumbnails of both endpoints, a live low-resolution preview movie you can scrub, an editable journey timeline, measured statistics, a help popover, and Cancel / default-button footer that swaps cleanly between settings, rendering and completion states.
  • The help content is unusually good. Every toolbar button is defined once together with the sentence that explains it, so a button cannot exist without an explanation, and the same sentence is the Mac tooltip, the iOS accessibility hint and the row in Controls.

The major themes needing work

  1. One shared sheet chrome, used unchanged on macOS. Places, Settings, Controls, Developer and Benchmarks are all NavigationStack { Form }.toolbar { Button("Done") }. On the Mac this produces a fixed ~380 pt panel that ignores the window, puts Done at the bottom left, offers no Cancel and no default button, does not respond to Escape, clips its content, and renders section headers as unstyled body text. One cause, five screens, a dozen symptoms.
  2. The iPhone Zoom Movie sheet is a different, much weaker product than the Mac one — a different route model (direct descent only), no preview, no endpoint thumbnails, no minimum-duration guard, no completion confirmation, and its settings stay fully interactive while a render is running.
  3. The app never tells you where you are. There is no zoom or coordinate readout anywhere in the exploration view on either platform; the only place to find it is inside the Places sheet, which covers the view you are asking about.
  4. Success is silent. Bookmarking gives no acknowledgement at all, and finishing a movie on iPhone gives none either. These are the two classic “did that work?” moments in the app.
  5. Accessibility falls over at large text sizes and the fractal itself is invisible to VoiceOver on both platforms.
  6. Half the shareable product is missing. There is no still-image export in the UI on either platform (only in the headless CLI), and Share shares a raw mandelbrot:// URL that nobody without the app can open.

The systemic finding

Roughly two thirds of the individual P1/P2 items resolve to four systemic causes: the shared sheet chrome, the absence of a form-layout convention on macOS, an unspecified slider/label pattern, and an unspecified way of formatting a zoom scale (this audit observed the same quantity printed as 1.0, 1×, 4e3×, 4000.0, 4,000× and 2.0000000000000004e9×). Fixing those four things once is worth more than fixing the symptoms screen by screen — see Design-system findings.

How this was produced

The macOS app was built Release and driven interactively with real mouse, keyboard and menu input at a 1280 × 800 window (and at 560 × 620 for the narrow case), captured window-only with screencapture -l. The iPhone build was installed on an iPhone 17 Pro simulator and driven by synthesised taps and drags against the live accessibility hierarchy, captured with xcrun simctl io … screenshot. Every statement below is tagged:

  • observed — seen happening in the running app.
  • source — read from the code but not reproduced in this session.
  • suspected — seen once, not reliably reproduced.

Three pieces of temporary state were used and have been removed: a set of seeded bookmarks written into the simulator's UserDefaults, and DeveloperToolsUnlocked / DeveloperMenuEnabled on the Mac. No source file was modified. See Coverage & limitations.

1. Main exploration view

The app's home. A full-bleed GPU canvas with a translucent control cluster; no chrome, no status bar on iPhone, no sidebar or inspector on Mac.

Strengths

  • Strength The content-to-chrome ratio is exemplary. On the Mac the toolbar is a single compact glass cluster at the trailing edge; on iPhone it is one capsule. Nothing else is drawn over the fractal at rest. observed
  • Strength Rendering quality is the product. Colour is smooth and band-free at every depth reached, refinement fades in rather than popping, and the canvas never showed a blank frame during panning or zooming. observed
  • Strength The geometry and input surface share the full drawable area while the controls live in the safe area as siblings, so opening or closing the companion never shifts the camera. This is a deliberate and correct decision. source, confirmed by observation

Concerns / Problems

  • Problem There is no location or zoom readout anywhere in the exploration view. After following a shared link to 2×10⁹, nothing on screen says where you are or how deep you have gone. The only readout is Centre / Scale inside the Places sheet — which covers the view. For an app whose entire subject is position and scale, that is the most conspicuous gap in the exploration UI. observed
  • Concern Jumping to a place is an instantaneous cut. Choosing Seahorse Valley from Places replaces the screen with no transition at all. The app has the machinery for a continuous camera move — it is exactly what the movie renderer does — but the interactive jump does not use it, so the user loses their sense of where they went. observed
  • Concern On iPhone the status bar is hidden (.statusBarHidden()). That is defensible for an immersive canvas, but it also removes the clock and battery for the whole session, and the control capsule sits directly under the Dynamic Island where the system would have drawn them. observed
  • Concern The app does not restore the last viewed location on launch. Three restored Mac windows all opened at the whole set. observed

Recommendations

  • Add a persistent, quiet scale readout. On macOS the natural home is the window subtitle (.navigationSubtitle), which is exactly what it is for: “−0.743643887037151, 0.13182590420533 · 4,000×”. On iPhone, a single small capsule at the bottom-leading corner showing just the zoom (“4,000×”), tappable to reveal the full coordinates.
  • Animate jumps to a place and to a bookmark as a short continuous zoom (0.5–0.8 s) rather than a cut, honouring Reduce Motion by falling back to a cross-fade.
  • Persist the last viewport in @SceneStorage so a relaunch returns the user where they were.

Priority

  • P1No scale or coordinate readout in the exploration view on either platform. Systemic — affects exploration, bookmarking and movie endpoint selection.
  • P2Place/bookmark restoration is a hard cut; no visual continuity.
  • P2Last location is not restored on launch.

2. macOS toolbar

Strengths

  • Strength Every button has a helpful, human tooltip — a full sentence, not a repeat of the label, and the same sentence appears in Controls. Hover highlighting is correct. observed
  • Strength Back and Forward are properly disabled when there is no history, and the Upright item appears only when the view is rotated. observed
  • Strength Overflow at narrow widths works and reveals labels. observed
  • Strength All ten items carry accessibility descriptions (Reset, Back, Forward, Places, Bookmark, Julia Companion, Zoom Movie, Share, Settings, Controls). observed via the AX tree

Concerns / Problems

  • Problem Two near-identical bookmark glyphs sit next to each other. bookmark means “open Places” and bookmark.fill means “bookmark this view”. Filled versus outline is the standard iOS/macOS encoding of state (bookmarked / not bookmarked), not of two different commands. A user will reasonably read the pair as one toggle. observed
  • Problem Adding a toolbar item shifts every other item. When history becomes available, or the view is rotated, Back/Upright are inserted and the whole cluster slides. Because the cluster is trailing-aligned, the buttons the user has learned the position of all move — and the change is triggered by the very action that precedes the next click. observed on both platforms
  • Concern Ten equal-weight icon buttons with no grouping. Navigation (Reset/Back/Forward/Upright), library (Places/Bookmark), view (Julia), and output (Movie/Share) are four different families rendered identically, with no ToolbarItemGroup, no spacer and no .navigation placement for back/forward. observed
  • Concern The toolbar is not user-customisable (.toolbar(id:) / customizationBehavior), and overflow priority is purely positional, so at narrow widths the app hides Share, Settings and Controls before it hides Forward. observed

Recommendations

  • Give the two library actions distinct glyphs. Use books.vertical or rectangle.grid.2x2 for Places (it is a gallery, not a bookmark) and keep bookmark/bookmark.fill as a single item whose fill reflects whether the current view is already bookmarked.
  • Reserve space for the conditional items, or move Back/Forward to a fixed ToolbarItemGroup(placement: .navigation) at the leading edge — the Mac convention — so the trailing cluster never moves.
  • Group the trailing cluster with ToolbarItemGroup and a ToolbarSpacer between families, and set customizationBehavior so overflow drops the least important items first.

Priority

  • P1Two adjacent bookmark glyphs for two different commands.
  • P1Conditional toolbar items reflow the whole cluster. Also affects iPhone.
  • P2No grouping or overflow priority among ten equal-weight items.

3. iPhone control cluster

Strengths

  • Strength Each button is a genuine 44 × 44 pt target with .contentShape(Rectangle()), confirmed in the accessibility hierarchy, and each carries a label and a hint. observed
  • Strength A single capsule of translucent material reads as one object and keeps the image uninterrupted. observed

Concerns / Problems

  • Problem The controls are at the top of a large phone. On an iPhone 17 Pro the cluster sits at y ≈ 100 pt — the least reachable part of the screen for a one-handed grip, and the app is explicitly a one-handed, pinch-and-explore product. In landscape it is worse. observed
  • Problem Neither Bookmark nor Share exists on iPhone. The Mac has both as toolbar items; on iPhone bookmarking is only reachable by opening Places, scrolling to a text field and tapping a 20 pt-tall button, and sharing only from inside Places. The phone is the platform where people actually share. observed
  • Problem The cluster's material is content-dependent. Over the bright orange/gold regions the capsule becomes a pale tan and the dark glyphs are fine; over dark regions it goes near-black and contrast drops. There is no fixed minimum contrast between glyph and backing. observed
  • Concern Six to seven unlabelled icons is the app's entire affordance surface on iPhone and there is no way to see their names short of opening Controls. observed

Recommendations

  • Move the cluster to the bottom-trailing safe area, mirroring where the companion inset already lives, so both live in the thumb arc. Keep the top clear for the eventual scale readout.
  • Add Bookmark and Share to the iPhone cluster. If seven is too many, collapse Settings + Controls into one “…” menu — those are the two the user visits least.
  • Reserve the Back slot (render it disabled rather than absent) so positions are stable.
  • Give the capsule an explicit minimum-contrast treatment: a fixed .ultraThinMaterial over a subtle scrim, or a constant tint, rather than letting the fractal dictate legibility.

Priority

  • P1Primary controls sit in the hardest-to-reach corner of a large phone.
  • P1Bookmark and Share have no iPhone entry point outside a sheet.
  • P2Glyph contrast against the capsule varies with the fractal behind it.

4. macOS menus and keyboard

Strengths

  • Strength A full command set exists and is defined once, so the Controls sheet, the tooltips and the menu can never drift apart. Most bindings are conventional (⌘+, ⌘−, ⌘D, ⌘J, ⌘M). observed
  • Strength The Debug menu is correctly hidden behind a held Option key or a launch flag. observed

Concerns / Problems

  • Problem No Settings… item and no ⌘, anywhere. The app menu contains only About, Services, Hide, Hide Others, Show All, Quit. Settings is reachable only from a toolbar icon. ⌘, is one of the most reflexive shortcuts on the platform. observed
  • Problem No menu item validates. Back and Forward are enabled with an empty history (the toolbar versions correctly are not); Swap Main and Companion is enabled with the companion hidden; Upright is enabled when the view is already upright. Choosing them does nothing and gives no feedback. observed
  • Problem Twenty-one items in one flat list. Navigation, zoom, rotation, detail, library, companion and movie are all one undivided run. Mac menus are expected to be grouped by separators. observed
  • Concern Reset View is ⇧H — a bare Shift shortcut with no command key, unlike every other item. It will also fire while a text field is focused in a sheet. source; the menu confirms the binding
  • Concern The four arrow keys are registered as unmodified menu shortcuts (Move Left/Right/Up/Down). Any text field elsewhere in the app now shares its arrow keys with a menu command. source — not reproduced; flagged as a risk
  • Concern Controls shows no shortcut in the menu even though the help advertises ?. observed
  • Concern The File menu contains only New Window and Close — no Export, no Save Image, no Open Location, and Share is absent from the menus entirely. observed

Recommendations

  • Add a Settings scene (or a CommandGroup(replacing: .appSettings) item) so ⌘, opens the existing Settings UI.
  • Drive every command's enabled state off the focused ExplorerModel — .disabled(!(explorer?.canGoBack ?? false)) and so on. The state is already published for the toolbar.
  • Split Explore into grouped sections with Divider(): Navigate · Zoom & Rotate · Detail · Library · Companion · Movie. Consider moving zoom/rotation/detail to a View menu, which is where a Mac user looks for them.
  • Give Reset View a command-key shortcut (⌘0 or ⌘1 are the conventional “back to 100 %” bindings) and drop the bare-Shift binding.
  • Add Export Image… (⇧⌘E) and Share to the File menu once still export exists (see §9).

Priority

  • P1No Settings… / ⌘, in the app menu.
  • P1No menu item validation — commands that cannot work appear available.
  • P2Twenty-one ungrouped items; no View menu.
  • P2Unmodified arrow-key shortcuts and a bare-Shift Reset binding risk conflicting with text entry.
  • P2Controls shortcut not shown in the menu.

5. Gestures and direct manipulation

This is the heart of the product and the criterion where it scores highest.

Strengths

  • Strength One transform, not three recognisers. On iPhone, pan, pinch and twist are solved together from the two raw touches: rotate and scale about the previous midpoint, then translate it — the unique transform that pins both fingers. Three independent recognisers could not agree on a single result, and the difference is felt. source; motion confirmed smooth in the simulator
  • Strength Inertia is handled with care: a quick lift keeps its fling, a held finish does not; trackpad momentum is left to AppKit rather than having a second inertia curve added on top; scroll interaction is only considered finished when momentum ends, so automatic depth waits for the view to settle. These are the details that separate smooth from nearly smooth. source
  • Strength The Mac gesture set is properly Mac: drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom at the pointer, ⌘-scroll to zoom on a trackpad, two-finger twist with a snap near a right angle, double-click to zoom, shift-drag to frame a region. The iPhone set is properly iPhone: pinch, double-tap in, two-finger tap out. Neither is a translation of the other. observed on macOS for drag/scroll/double-click; shift-drag framing from source
  • Strength The crosshair is drawn above the input surface but takes no hits; the input view owns the pointer and grabs the marker itself, so there is no gesture conflict between moving c and panning. A click that barely moves pins instead of dragging. source; hover tracking observed on macOS

Concerns / Problems

  • Concern Shift-drag framing is undiscoverable. It is the most precise way to navigate and it exists only as a line in the Controls sheet. There is no cursor change on Shift, no hint, and no equivalent on iPhone at all. source + Controls sheet; not exercised (the automation harness cannot hold a modifier during a drag)
  • Concern The companion panel eats the bottom-right corner of the gesture surface. On iPhone the inset is 169 × 169 pt with its own input view, so panning that starts in the lower-right quadrant drives the companion instead of the main view. There is no visual signal that the gesture surface has a hole in it. observed (panel geometry and its own input view)
  • Concern Double-click zoom may fire on well-separated clicks. During one sequence, two clicks about 800 pt apart with a sheet interaction between them left the view zoomed 2× with the history advanced. This could not be reproduced deliberately. suspected — one occurrence, not confirmed
  • Concern There is no “zoom out one step” gesture on macOS to match iPhone's two-finger tap; the Mac needs the keyboard or the scroll wheel. source

Recommendations

  • Surface shift-drag: change the cursor to a crosshair while Shift is held over the canvas, and show the framed rectangle's resulting zoom factor as you drag.
  • Make the iPhone companion inset visibly a separate surface — a thin border and a shadow already exist; add a small drag handle, and let a pan that begins on the panel's edge fall through to the main view. Better still, let the user move the inset between corners.
  • Add ⌥-double-click (or a two-finger double-tap) as zoom-out on macOS, and document it in Controls.
  • If the double-click behaviour is reproducible, require both clicks to fall within the system double-click distance before treating clickCount == 2 as a zoom.

Priority

  • P2Shift-drag framing has no discoverable affordance.
  • P2The iPhone companion inset silently removes a corner of the exploration surface.
  • P2Investigate whether a double-click zoom can fire from two distant clicks. Unconfirmed.

6. Julia companion

Strengths

  • Strength On macOS, hover-driven c is genuinely delightful and is the clearest expression of the product's idea. It is fast enough to feel continuous. observed
  • Strength The panel has its own pan, pinch and twist, and its own reset, so it is a real second viewer rather than a thumbnail. source; reset and swap buttons observed
  • Strength The pin metaphor is right: drag the crosshair to move it, click it to pin, pinned is filled. Simple and physical. source; pin button observed
  • Strength Swapping promotes the Julia set to the main view cleanly and the platform layout adapts correctly (side-by-side on Mac and iPad-class widths, corner inset on a compact phone). observed

Concerns / Problems

  • Problem On iPhone the companion opens disconnected from the view. Because there is no pointer to track, juliaMarker is nil until you touch the canvas, so the panel appears showing the built-in default c = −0.8 + 0.156i — a point nowhere near the current view — with no crosshair to explain the mismatch. The single most important idea of the feature (“this is the Julia set for that point”) is not communicated at the moment the feature is turned on. observed
  • Problem The c readout is fixed at four decimal places. At 4×10³ it is already coarse; at 2×10⁹ every point on screen reads -0.7436+0.1318i, so the caption conveys nothing and, worse, implies the companion is not changing. It should carry at least enough digits to distinguish adjacent pixels at the current scale. observed
  • Problem The panel's three buttons are 26 × 26 pt — well under the 44 pt minimum — and on macOS they are grey circles on black with very low contrast. observed in the AX hierarchy and screenshots
  • Problem The whole panel is one accessibility label. The container, its duplicate, all three buttons and the caption text all report identifier: companionPanel, label: "Julia companion". VoiceOver announces four identical “Julia companion” buttons and cannot read the value of c. observed in the AX hierarchy
  • Concern In swapped mode the crosshair and the c caption both disappear, and the Mandelbrot inset is a non-interactive thumbnail — so you cannot pick a new c without swapping back. observed
  • Concern The companion's own viewport starts at scale 1 in a tall panel, so a lot of the panel is empty black. It should fit the set to the panel. observed on macOS
  • Concern Rotating the phone loses the crosshair. observed

macOS notes

Opening the companion narrows the Mandelbrot view from the right, cropping content rather than rescaling it. That is defensible (it preserves scale), but there is no visible Divider() between the two — it is black on black — so the split reads as one image until the Julia set appears.

iPhone notes

The inset is well placed for the thumb, but it is a fixed corner, it cannot be resized or moved, and it overlaps the exploration surface (see §5).

Recommendations

  • On iPhone, seed the marker at the centre of the view when the companion is first shown, so the crosshair and the companion always agree with each other and with the current location.
  • Format c from the current scale: enough significant figures to separate one screen pixel from the next, capped sensibly, in a monospaced style so it does not jitter.
  • Raise the panel buttons to 44 pt hit targets (the visible circle can stay small via .contentShape) and give them a material backing for contrast.
  • Remove the container-level accessibilityLabel/accessibilityIdentifier from CompanionPanel and label the three buttons individually — the correct strings already exist as their help text. Expose the caption's real text.
  • Make the Mandelbrot inset interactive in swapped mode (tap to move c), and keep the c readout visible in both modes.
  • Fit the companion's initial viewport to its panel aspect.

Priority

  • P1On iPhone the companion opens on an unrelated default c with no crosshair — the feature's core idea is not communicated.
  • P1Companion panel elements are all announced as “Julia companion”; the caption is unreadable to VoiceOver.
  • P1Panel controls are 26 pt touch targets.
  • P2Fixed 4-decimal c readout is meaningless below about 10⁴×.
  • P2Swapped mode drops the crosshair and the c readout, and the inset is inert.
  • P2Companion viewport does not fit its panel; divider invisible on macOS.

7. Places (gallery and bookmarks)

Strengths

  • Strength Putting “this view”, the user's bookmarks and a curated gallery in one place is the right information architecture — the user does not have to know whether they are saving or loading. observed
  • Strength The famous-places gallery is well chosen and each entry carries its own palette, so it doubles as a palette showcase. observed
  • Strength Per-row share buttons let you send a place without visiting it. observed

Concerns / Problems

  • Problem This is a picture app whose library has no pictures. Every row is two lines of text. The app already contains LocationThumbnail — it renders exactly this, and the Mac movie sheet uses it for its endpoints — but Places does not. observed
  • Problem The sheet does not use the Mac's space. It stayed the same size when the window went from 560 pt to 1280 pt wide, it is not resizable, and it clips the list mid-row (“Mini Mandelbrot” is sliced in half). Six of nine gallery entries are visible. observed at both window sizes
  • Problem “Done” is at the bottom leading edge and there is no Cancel and no default button. On macOS the confirming button belongs bottom-trailing; on iOS it belongs in the navigation bar (where it is, correctly, on iPhone). The Mac inherits the iOS placement and then loses it to the bottom-left. observed
  • Problem No empty state. With no bookmarks the section vanishes silently. A first-run user has no idea the feature exists. observed
  • Problem Bookmarks and the built-in gallery are indistinguishable apart from the section header, and there is no ordering control, no rename, no edit, and no detail view. LocationStore.rename exists in the model but has no UI. Deletion is swipe-only and therefore invisible on macOS. observed; rename from source
  • Problem The same quantity is formatted two ways in one sheet. “This view · Scale” shows 4000.0 while the gallery row for the same place shows 4e3×, and a bookmark of the home view shows 1.0× against the gallery's 1×. observed
  • Concern A row separator under the “Name this view / Bookmark” row is inset incorrectly and stops under the button, leaving a visible stub. observed on iPhone

Recommendations

  • Give each row a rendered thumbnail using the existing LocationThumbnail, and on macOS use a grid (a gallery of images) rather than a list — that is what the extra space is for.
  • Let the sheet grow: replace the fixed .frame(minWidth: 380, minHeight: 460) with a resizable presentation sized to the window, and give the list a proper scroll area with no clipped row.
  • Move the confirming button to the bottom-trailing edge on macOS with .keyboardShortcut(.defaultAction), and make Escape dismiss (see Design-system findings).
  • Add an empty state: one line of explanation and a “Bookmark this view” button, so Places teaches the feature.
  • Add rename (it already exists in the store), reorder, and a context menu with Rename / Share / Delete — which also gives macOS a discoverable delete.
  • Route every scale through one formatter (see Design-system findings).

Priority

  • P1No thumbnails in the library of an image app, although the renderer for them already exists.
  • P1Sheet ignores window size and clips its content on macOS. Systemic — shared sheet chrome.
  • P1No empty state for bookmarks.
  • P1No rename / reorder / context menu; delete is swipe-only and so unreachable on macOS.
  • P2Scale formatted two different ways within one sheet. Systemic.
  • P2Misaligned row separator under the naming row.

8. Bookmark creation and feedback

Three entry points: the text field inside Places, the toolbar's filled-bookmark button, and ⌘D.

Concerns / Problems

  • Problem Bookmarking is completely silent from the toolbar and from ⌘D. bookmarkCurrentView() adds the place and nothing else happens: no animation, no toast, no change to the toolbar icon, no sheet. The user has no way to know it worked short of opening Places. This is the app's clearest “did that work?” moment. source; the toolbar path was not separately re-tested, the in-sheet path was
  • Problem The first click on “Bookmark” after typing a name did nothing — it moved focus and re-selected the field text. A second click created the bookmark. A user who types a name, clicks once and sees nothing will reasonably conclude the feature is broken. observed once; reproduced only in that one sequence, so treat the exact trigger as unconfirmed
  • Problem The default name is machine-generated and ugly. With no name typed, the bookmark is called "\(scaleDescription.prefix(12))× view" — “1.0× view” at home, and at depth something like “2.000000000× view”. observed
  • Concern The bookmark list is a nested ObservableObject (let bookmarks: LocationStore) that PlacesView does not observe, so a change to it is not guaranteed to refresh the view. In this session the list did update, but only after a second interaction re-evaluated the body. source; the delayed update was observed but has an alternative explanation

Workflow review

A. Exploration — launch → pan → zoom → refine → continue

Observed: launch is immediate and the whole set renders at once. Dragging, scroll-zoom at the pointer, double-click zoom and pinch all work smoothly and detail fades in. Back/Forward become available as you move, and history is recorded when a view settles and differs meaningfully — a well-judged rule that avoids a history full of near-duplicates.

Friction: the moment you stop, you cannot say where you are. There is no scale readout, so “continue exploring” has no anchor; deciding whether this is somewhere worth bookmarking means opening a sheet that covers it. On iPhone, the Back chevron appearing mid-session shifts every other control.

Especially successful: the gesture model. Nothing in this workflow feels mediated.

B. Bookmarking — find a place → bookmark → browse → restore

Observed: On Mac, clicking the filled-bookmark toolbar button appears to do nothing at all. Opening Places, typing a name and clicking Bookmark required two clicks before the entry appeared. The new bookmark then sits in a text-only list next to nine built-in places that look identical to it, with its scale formatted differently from theirs. Restoring it is an instant cut to somewhere else.

Friction, in order of cost: no confirmation → no thumbnail → no rename → no transition. A user cannot tell that the first step worked, and then cannot recognise the result.

State loss: restoring a bookmark silently replaces the current palette, contrast, phase and iteration limit. That is probably correct, but it is unannounced and not undoable except via Back.

C. Julia exploration — Mandelbrot location → Julia → explore → return

Observed on macOS: excellent. Toggle the companion, move the pointer, and the Julia set changes under it; click the crosshair to pin; the panel pans and zooms on its own; swap to promote the Julia set; swap back. Conceptual continuity is genuinely preserved.

Observed on iPhone: the same toggle produces a Julia set for a point you did not choose, with no crosshair anywhere, and the caption gives four decimal places of a number that needs ten. The connection between the two views — the entire point — is not made until you happen to touch the canvas.

Missing step: there is no way to bookmark, share or render a movie of a Julia set. It is a view, not a place, so it falls outside the rest of the app's vocabulary.

D. Movie creation — choose start → choose end → configure → preview → render → completion → share

Observed on macOS: the best workflow in the app. Open the dialog, see both endpoints as pictures, read a sentence describing the route, watch a real preview of it, open Edit Journey to slow a leg or pull the overview back, read measured statistics, choose a folder, render with two live counters and a Cancel, and finish on a card that tells you what was made and where. The only real breaks are the blank From popup at the start and the completion card being below the fold at the end.

Observed on iPhone: the same intent, a different product. The end is fixed to the current view (so “choose end” is not a step), the start list offers no pictures, a raw float is shown, there is no preview, no timeline, no statistics, no guard against an impossible duration, and completion is announced by a progress bar disappearing.

Cross-screen friction: on both platforms the movie's end is “this view”, so the whole workflow depends on the user having already navigated to the right place — but the exploration view never told them where that was, and the movie sheet is the first time they see it named.

E. Export — current visual state → controls → progress → result → share

This workflow does not exist. There is no still-image export in the UI on either platform. The only export is a movie, and the only share is a custom-scheme URL. A user who has found something beautiful can screenshot it or nothing.

F. Recovery and cancellation

Movie rendering can be cancelled from a prominent control on both platforms. Navigation has Back/Forward with a well-judged recording rule. Beyond that there is no undo: deleting a bookmark is immediate and irreversible (and unreachable on macOS), changing the palette or detail cannot be undone, and restoring a bookmark's colours cannot be reverted except by navigating back.

Cross-platform consistency

The goal is “the same application, expressed naturally in each platform's language”. The app achieves that in the places where someone clearly sat down and designed for each platform, and misses it everywhere the shared Form was simply shipped to both.

Expressed correctly on both

  • Gestures. Two genuinely different, genuinely idiomatic sets, with two different help lists. The single best example of the principle in the codebase.
  • Layout adaptation. Side-by-side companion by horizontal size class; corner inset on a compact phone; Mac gets a window toolbar, iPhone gets a floating capsule.
  • Movie output destination. macOS gets a chosen, security-scoped folder that survives relaunch; iOS correctly has no folder and uses the share sheet. The code says so in a comment, and it is the right call.
  • Resolution options. 4K on Mac, capped at 1080p on iOS.
  • Keyboard. Shown on macOS always; on iOS only when a hardware keyboard is actually present, and it watches for connection changes while the sheet is open.

Desktop design leaked onto iPhone

  • Settings' companion section says “Follow the pointer” and “click it to pin” on a phone.
  • The primary controls are at the top of the screen — a window-toolbar position, not a phone position.
  • Places and Settings are the Mac's list structure with an iOS skin: the same nine-row gallery, the same row-level share buttons, the same inline “Bookmark” button rendered at 20 pt tall.

Mobile design enlarged onto macOS

  • Every sheet is NavigationStack { Form } with a navigation title and a Done toolbar button. On macOS that yields a phone-width panel, a title bar the platform does not use, Done at the bottom left, no Cancel, no default button, no Escape, and content clipped on both edges.
  • NavigationLink to Acknowledgements pushes inside a Mac sheet and removes its dismissal control.
  • Swipe-to-delete is the only way to remove a bookmark — a gesture macOS does not have.

Workflows that differ for no defensible reason

  • Movie creation — two different route models and two different feature sets from two separate files. The Mac's journey planning, preview, timeline, statistics and completion card are all absent on iPhone. Nothing about the phone makes those impossible; the preview is 480 × 270 at 12 fps.
  • Bookmark and Share — first-class toolbar items on Mac, buried in a sheet on iPhone.
  • Render feedback — two counters, a percentage and dimmed settings on Mac; one counter and live settings on iPhone.

Recommendation

Accessibility and resilience

Tested in this session

  • Problem The iPhone control capsule and the companion panel break at accessibility text sizes. At AX5 the glyphs grow past their fixed 44 × 44 pt frames and overlap one another and the capsule background; the companion's three 26 pt buttons do the same; and the “Julia c = …” caption capsule expands until it hides the entire Julia set. The buttons remain in the accessibility tree at their original coordinates, so what the user sees no longer corresponds to what they can tap. observed at accessibility-extra-extra-large
  • Strength Every Form-based sheet survives AX5 intact — Settings reflowed with labels above controls, no truncation and no clipping. That is a real, tested strength and it comes from using standard controls. observed
  • Problem The fractal is invisible to VoiceOver on macOS. The window's accessibility tree is a toolbar of ten well-labelled buttons plus a single unlabelled group containing nothing. There is no description, no value, no actions. observed via the AX tree
  • Concern On iPhone the canvas is one opaque element labelled “Mandelbrot explorer”, with no value, no traits and no custom actions — so a VoiceOver user can know the explorer is there but cannot learn where they are, or pan and zoom. observed via the AX hierarchy
  • Problem The companion panel reports five elements with one label. The container, its duplicate, all three buttons and the caption all announce “Julia companion”. observed via the AX hierarchy
  • Problem Touch targets below 44 pt: companion panel buttons 26 × 26 pt; the “Bookmark” button in Places 77 × 20 pt. observed via the AX hierarchy
  • Strength All ten macOS toolbar buttons and all seven iPhone cluster buttons carry correct accessibility labels, and the iPhone buttons additionally carry the explanatory sentence as a hint. observed
  • Concern No keyboard focus was visible anywhere on macOS outside text fields; the canvas accepts first responder for key commands but shows no focus ring, and there is no Tab order through the toolbar. observed

Inferred from source, not tested

  • Reduce Motion is not consulted anywhere. There is no accessibilityReduceMotion reference in the UI code, yet the app has inertial flings, spring-based rotation snapping, refinement fades and a rotation animation. At minimum, flings and the refinement fade should be shortened when Reduce Motion is on.
  • Colour carries meaning without a second channel in two places: the green tick on the Mac completion card, and the red/orange error and warning captions. Both have accompanying text, which mitigates it; the movie timeline's segment icons (magnifier vs arrows) are a good example of doing it right.
  • Localisation resilience is untested. Strings are inline literals rather than a string catalogue, and several layouts depend on short English labels — the movie sheet's 52 pt “From:”/“To:” gutter, the Mac settings' fixed label column, the 116 pt thumbnail captions. German or French would likely clip several of them. No localisation was attempted in this session.
  • The crosshair is white with a 2 pt shadow and is drawn over content that is frequently white. It was legible over mid-tones in testing; over the bright interior bands it will not be.

Recommendations

  • Cap the control glyphs and the companion caption with .dynamicTypeSize(...DynamicTypeSize.accessibility1) (or size them with @ScaledMetric against a ceiling) so the capsule stays coherent, and let the frames grow with the glyph rather than clipping.
  • Make the canvas an accessible element with a live value — “Centred on −0.743…, 0.131…, zoomed 4,000 times” — and add accessibilityAdjustableAction for zoom plus custom actions for pan, so VoiceOver users can explore. On macOS, give the NSView an accessibilityRole, label and value.
  • Remove the container-level label on CompanionPanel and label its children.
  • Raise the two sub-44 pt targets.
  • Honour Reduce Motion for flings, fades and the rotation spring.
  • Give the crosshair a dark outline or a blend mode so it is legible on any background.
  • Move user-facing strings into a string catalogue before the layouts harden further.

Design-system findings

Most of this report collapses into five repeated causes. Fixing these is worth more than fixing the symptoms individually.

1. The shared sheet chrome P0 P1

Five screens — Places, Settings, Controls, Developer, Benchmarks — are NavigationStack { Form/List }.navigationTitle(…).toolbar { Button("Done") } with a hard-coded .frame(minWidth:…, idealWidth:…, minHeight:…). On iPhone this is correct and idiomatic. On macOS it produces, every time:

  • a fixed panel that ignores the window and cannot be resized;
  • content clipped at the edges or through the middle of a row;
  • Done at the bottom leading edge;
  • no Cancel and no default button, so Return does nothing;
  • Escape does not dismiss (verified on Settings, both at the root and after pushing to Acknowledgements);
  • a navigation title bar macOS sheets do not use;
  • Section headers rendered as unstyled body text;
  • an inconsistent label gutter, so some controls align to it and some do not.

Fix once: introduce a single PanelScaffold that is NavigationStack { Form } on iOS and, on macOS, a VStack with a heading, a scrolling Form().formStyle(.grouped) sized to the window, and a footer with Cancel plus a .keyboardShortcut(.defaultAction) confirm — the shape MovieSheetMac already uses. Settings should additionally become a real Settings scene reachable by ⌘,.

2. No slider or labelled-control convention P1

Sliders appear three times and are built three ways: a LabeledContent row followed by a bare Slider row (Exterior contrast, with a value); a bare Text followed by a bare Slider (Palette phase, with no value); and a labelled slider with an inline value in the movie timeline's Overview. None has minimum/maximum labels. Steppers are similarly inconsistent: label-in-row on iPhone, hidden-label-plus-separate-value on Mac, and hidden-label-plus-value-plus-minimum in the timeline — three numbers for one quantity.

Fix once: one ValueSlider(title:value:in:format:) and one ValueStepper used everywhere, each showing title, current value and bounds.
